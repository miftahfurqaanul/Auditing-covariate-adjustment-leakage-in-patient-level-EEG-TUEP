# Analysis plan, round 36: diagnosis of the leakage mechanism of window-level adjustment (2026-10-22)

## Background
The V4 covariate adjustment leaks in classifiers that use window/second-level features (Rohira null 0.573/0.562, Tang-F 0.605/0.596; V4t does not fix it) but not in patient-level features (I2 0.504/0.517, P3-V5 0.498/0.500, DNM 0.500/0.514, frozen embeddings 0.500-0.509). The set-mismatch hypothesis (V4t) is REJECTED. This round tests two new hypotheses with Rohira as the test instrument (CPU, cheap), before fixing NM and Tang-F.
## Analysis (written before the run)
In the null test (windows exchanged between patients; label, age, sex, era belong to the new owner), the only path that carries covariate information to the features is the correction term -D_p B_hat, which is fixed per patient. If the rows are i.i.d. and Gaussian, B_hat (the projection onto the design space) and the residuals (the orthogonal projection) are independent, so any classifier trained on the residuals is unaligned with B_hat and the mean null AUC is 0.5. Violations of this assumption cause leakage:
- H_A (weighting/clustering): window-level OLS weights by n_p (12-411 windows per patient) although the noise is clustered per patient (the variance of the patient mean = tau^2 + sigma^2/n_p, almost independent of n_p if tau^2 dominates). The weights are not the inverse of the variance, so B_hat and the residuals are not independent. Prediction: B_hat from the per-patient mean (one row per patient, unweighted; "pat") removes the leakage; logistic regression on the patient means still leaks if the per-window B_hat ("win") is used (so classifier capacity is not the main cause).
- H_B (in-sample residual shift): the residuals of training patients are computed with a B_hat fitted on them (the noise component correlated with D is removed in-sample) whereas the residuals of test patients are not; a high-capacity classifier learns the pattern of the training residuals and exploits that difference. Prediction: cross-fitting (the residual of each training patient uses B_hat from the other training folds; test patients use all) reduces the leakage, especially for RF.
## Design
Rohira (set A, 48 features, 28,982 windows), null H1 (global permutation) and H2a (within era) with seed 33000 + r (the first 10 identical to round 33; 10 replicates), without age/sex, fitting on all training patients. Cells:
- RF (Rohira-F recipe, 100 trees) on windows with adjustment: win (window-level OLS, as V4), pat (OLS on the unweighted patient mean), win_x and pat_x (5-fold cross-fit over training patients).
- Logistic regression (nested_lr) on patient means with adjustment win or pat (without cross-fit), and without adjustment as a control.
Rule R1 (round 30): mean <= 0.52 and mean + 1.645 SE <= 0.54 = no evidence of leakage; > 0.55 leakage. Reference: Rohira RF without adjustment 0.503/0.540 (round 33; H2a 0.54 reflects the era signal that this scenario retains); RF win 0.573/0.562.
## Predictions
- RF-pat: H1 and H2a <= 0.52 (55%). RF-win_x <= 0.52 (35%). RF-pat_x <= 0.52 (60%).
- LR-win (patient mean with per-window B_hat) H1 > 0.55 (40%); LR-pat <= 0.52 (85%).
- If RF-pat passes and LR-win leaks: H_A (weighting) is declared the main mechanism; if RF-pat leaks and LR-pat is clean: classifier capacity is a factor; if only cross-fit passes: H_B.
## Follow-up (fixed before the results)
Variants that pass R1 on Rohira are applied to NM (segment tokens) and Tang-F (second-level features), each with a null test before the real data, and F2 is recomputed if NM changes. No claim of superiority comes from this round.

## Addendum 1 (written AFTER seeing RF-pat H1 = 0.575 and the logistic regression cells; BEFORE the other RF cells finished and before the log cells; 2026-10-22)
Observation: RF-pat (coefficients from the unweighted patient mean) still leaks (H1 0.575, sd 0.027, n = 10), so H_A (window weighting) is REJECTED for RF. The logistic regression cells on patient means are at 0.508-0.524 (LR-none 0.484/0.525; LR-win 0.524/0.515; LR-pat 0.508/0.513), not clearly higher than the control.
New hypothesis H_C (heavy tails + nonlinear classifier): the Rohira features are raw band power (sum of the Welch PSD, microvolt-squared scale) with very heavy tails. The independence argument (B_hat and the residuals are independent) holds only for Gaussian data; for heavy-tailed data with a large influence of a few patients/windows, B_hat and the residuals are uncorrelated but not independent, and a nonlinear classifier can exploit that dependence whereas logistic regression is sensitive only to linear correlation. This explains why I2 (log relative power), the embeddings, and DNM are clean, NM (log-power tokens) is only slightly above, and Rohira (raw power) and Tang-F (PS features) leak with nonlinear classifiers.
Test: RF cells with log features (log band power, monotone, so RF without adjustment does not change) with pat and win adjustment: `RF-logpat`, `RF-logwin`; plus `RF-logpat_x` if cross-fit on the raw cells (RF-pat_x/RF-win_x) shows a reduction. Same seeds and replicates (33000 + r, 10 replicates; H1 and H2a). Same R1 rule.
Predictions (before the results): RF-logpat <= 0.52 on H1 (60%); RF-logwin <= 0.52 (50%); RF-win_x and RF-pat_x <= 0.52 (30% and 35%, revised downward because RF-pat leaks); if RF-logpat passes, H_C is supported and adjustment on log features is used for Rohira, NM, and Tang-F.
Additional diagnostics (no decision rule): kurtosis and skewness of raw vs log features on patient means and windows; the leverage fraction of the largest patient on B_hat.
